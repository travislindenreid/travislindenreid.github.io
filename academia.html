Academic Mission

Ever since I attended UC-Irvine's Active Learning Institute and earned their Course Design Certificate, I've been a huge proponent of flipped classrooms. I lecture very little- 5-30 minutes max per 3 hour class, usually including demos and Q&A- and instead spend class time on discussions and in-class activities. I create all of my game development class material from scratch, including my syllabi, lectures, tutorials, reading material, discussion activities, coding activities, and coding homework. My courses also foster student community through group activities and projects and through the use of student Learning Assistants in class.

I run a Director's Workshop for students who are running their own game teams to help them learn and practice creative direction skills in a supportive student community.

If you're a UCI student looking for resources, check out our class site for GDIM 31-32-33.

If you're looking for a teacher or professor for game development classes or a volunteer/mentor for a game dev event, don't hesitate to reach out to me. P.S: I would love to bring my experience as an indie game designer to classrooms and teach more game design, narrative design, visual design, and entrepreneurship courses. Also, my engineering specialization was tech art, and I'd LOVE to teach a class on it!

Service

My passion for community involvement started well before I considered a career in academia. I experienced the difficulties of being a gender minority in the Computer Science major and in the game development community in the American south during college, which I responded to by founding a student organization called Women in Computer Science. To this day, mentoring underrepresented communities in the game industry remains one of my top career priorities.
